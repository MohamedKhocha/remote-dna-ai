Remote DNA: teaching a TV the app has never seen

In the app, the phone sends one infrared code at a time and the user says what the TV did. Your browser cannot send infrared, so here the page plays the TV, using a real remote from the catalogue. That remote's brand is removed from everything the model can see, exactly as for a TV no list knows. Both sides stop when volume up, volume down, mute, OK and back are found.

      Deep search: one tap instead of thirty questions

      Before learning buttons, the app must find the address the TV listens on. When no remote in the catalogue reaches it, the phone tries the 720 addresses nobody uses, with number keys. It used to ask "did anything happen?" after every 24 addresses, up to 37 times. Now it sends them all without stopping, and you tap once when the channel number changes: the moment of the tap points to the few addresses on air just before it. Here the page hides a TV on one of those addresses. Press start, watch its screen, and tap.

        Find by model: no question at all

        Most TVs carry their model number on a label at the back. In the app, ML Kit reads a photo of it on the phone, offline, and the photo is deleted. Your browser cannot run that reader, so type the model or paste a label's text: the search below is the one the app runs on what it reads.

          The agent: rebuild a remote in a conversation

          The same tools as above, driven by a language model: it finds where the TV listens from its model number or brand, tests one button at a time, and fills in the rest with the DNA model. It never sends a code on its own: every send waits for your click, and it only counts a button once you say the TV reacted. Built in the last week of the programme with LangChain's agent course (agent/ in the repository, in Python); this page runs the same tools in JavaScript. Since 30 October it also runs inside the REMOTE DNA app, as a beta: the phone's own infrared emitter sends each code you approve, and the demo video shows it on a real TV.
